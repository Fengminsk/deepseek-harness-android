# Linux guest staging after process death

English | [中文](linux-guest-staging-recovery.zh.md)

An isolated Service may be killed after publishing a per-run rootfs and before its normal `Staged.close()` cleanup. A later isolated Service may have a different UID. `LinuxGuestStagingPreflight` does not assume that the later UID can open or delete the previous tree, and it never broadens private-directory permissions to enable cleanup. Before asking the App to claim and copy the sealed image, and again before unpacking it, the Service requires a directly accessible cache directory, rejects any `linux-guest-root-*` sibling, and checks that an empty sibling can be created and removed. An inaccessible cache or a leftover run therefore rejects staging; it does not silently reuse or delete the tree. Sequential retries cannot keep publishing new trees while a visible orphan remains. This is failure containment, not recovery of the occupied bytes.

This preflight is necessary but insufficient for the current design. The [Android 16 isolated app SELinux policy](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/system/sepolicy/+/android16-release/private/isolated_app_all.te) forbids an isolated process from opening App data files, even though already-open App data FDs may be used. The [App policy](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/system/sepolicy/+/android16-release/prebuilts/api/202404/private/app.te) excludes isolated processes from normal App private directory/file creation. The current `cacheDir` stage is therefore expected to fail on a stock Android 16 device; a successful Java host test or Android compile is no evidence that guest data can be materialized. Whether a different, policy-compliant per-run storage mechanism exists must be proven on the target device and through the actual SELinux and UID behavior.

The Service has no native PRoot launch, no model-facing Shell and no task-success publication. A safe later implementation must specify storage ownership and cleanup after forced process death, avoid unrestricted App-private access by the guest, and prove both normal and killed-Service paths on device before enabling executable userland. The existing negative Binder instrumentation checks identity and malformed handoff but cannot establish storage recovery. Host check: `node apps/android/tool/acceptance-linux-guest-staging-preflight.mjs`.
